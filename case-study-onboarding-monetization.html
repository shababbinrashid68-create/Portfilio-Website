User onboarding and monetization dashboard

SQL analysis in Google BigQuery and a Looker Studio dashboard for WYZR Friends, a live product built by Kainotomia Pte Ltd.

Role
Analysis, SQL and dashboard build
Client type
Internal product team
Tools
Google BigQuery (SQL), Looker Studio
Outcome
Answered part of the business question; limited by the absence of real monetization data

Context

The project manager of WYZR Friends asked for an analysis of user onboarding and monetization. WYZR Friends is a live product built by my company, Kainotomia Pte Ltd, so the work used real user data, with the team’s permission.

The dashboard was built to answer four primary business questions:

  • Who is driving potential revenue? Breakdown of monthly recurring revenue Simulated Scaled by age band and relationship status, showing that individuals aged 50–64 generate 45% of simulated MRR.
  • Where is onboarding most successful? Top 10 US states by profile completion rate, showing that users in states such as Georgia, Washington and Oregon complete their profiles at rates exceeding 94%.
  • Which sign-up methods yield the highest completion? Comparison of onboarding completion by authentication method, showing that Google sign-ups reach the highest profile completion (60.3%), while Apple ID trails at 49.68%.
  • What is the overall health of the onboarding funnel? Headline view of total sign-ups (32,078 Scaled) versus fully onboarded users (18,138 Scaled), resulting in a 56.5% overall profile completion rate.

My role

I wrote the SQL queries behind the onboarding and monetization dashboard in Google BigQuery and built the dashboard in Looker Studio. I framed the business questions, defined the metrics, made the data-governance decisions described below, and validated the outputs.

Approach

Step 1 Rebuilding the age field

The stored age field was about 40% empty, and its bands overlapped. I recalculated age from date of birth instead, grouping users into five bands (30–39, 40–49, 50–64, 65–74 and 75+). [VERIFY]

Decision

Recalculating from date of birth gives one consistent definition of age, rather than patching a field with gaps and overlapping bands. [VERIFY]

Step 2 Setting a minimum segment size

Segments with fewer than 50 users were excluded because rates from groups that small were too unstable to compare. On the dashboard, this rule excluded states with fewer than 50 users, the Email & Password sign-up method (n < 50), and the 30–39 and 75+ Couple cohorts.

Trade-off

Some small groups drop out of the view. The rates that remain can be compared with each other. [VERIFY]

Step 3 Modelling revenue as scenarios Simulated

I had no permission to use monetization data, so the revenue component is a simulation Simulated. I modelled a hypothetical $9.99/month premium tier with subscriber propensity derived from profile completion, and built a Looker Studio parameter (MRR Scenario) that switches monthly recurring revenue estimates between conservative, base and optimistic scenarios.

Decision

Three labelled scenarios show a range and keep the assumption visible. A single simulated number would read as a forecast. [VERIFY]

Step 4 Comparing profile completion by sign-up method and state

Across 32,078 sign-ups Scaled, 18,138 users Scaled completed onboarding, an overall profile completion rate of 56.5%. By sign-up method (with Email & Password excluded for n < 50), Google led at 60.3% and Phone OTP reached 56.38%, while Apple ID trailed Google by 10.6 percentage points at 49.68%. Across US states with at least 50 users, Georgia led at 99.32% and all top-10 states exceeded 94%—a skew I noted on the dashboard because state is captured during profile setup rather than at initial sign-up.

Decision

Noting on the chart that state is captured at profile setup prevents readers from mistaking 94%+ state completion rates for top-of-funnel sign-up conversion. [VERIFY]

Data governance and constraints

Working with real product records required setting boundaries on what entered the queries and what could be shown outside the team.

Data-governance summary

Governance rules
Permissions
All permissions were obtained from the WYZR Friends team before the data was used.
Personal data Excluded
Email and phone-number fields were deliberately excluded so personal identifiers never entered the reporting layer.
User volumes Scaled
All user volumes (such as 32,078 sign-ups and 18,138 fully onboarded users) are scaled with multipliers. No real user counts appear anywhere.
Revenue component Simulated
I had no permission to use monetization data, so all revenue estimates (such as the $57,719/month Base MRR) are simulated (Simulated) across conservative, base and optimistic scenarios using a hypothetical $9.99/month tier.
Live dashboard access Not linked
The live Looker Studio dashboard is never linked or embedded. It contains production queries and carries risk to the client.

Outcome

The dashboard showed an overall profile completion rate of 56.5% (18,138 fully onboarded users out of 32,078 sign-ups Scaled), surfaced a 10.6 percentage-point completion gap between Apple ID (49.68%) and Google sign-in (60.3%), and concentrated 45% of simulated Base MRR ($26,186 of $57,719/month Simulated Scaled) among individual users aged 50–64. Overall, the analysis answered part of the business question on onboarding behaviour, while its monetization findings remained illustrative because real monetization data was not available.

What I learned

Most of the work happens before the query is written, and an analysis is only as strong as the data it is allowed to see.

What I would do differently: agree data access, including monetization data, with the project team before fixing the scope of the questions. [VERIFY]
