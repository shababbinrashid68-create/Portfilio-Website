AI output audit for an OSINT threat-intelligence platform

Evaluating an in-house AI module's threat scores, red flags, and narrative summaries, and separating model-output failures from functional software bugs.

Role
AI model evaluation, functional bug testing and product improvement input
Client type
In-house product (Parallaxlogic Infotech)
Tools
Google Sheets, Gemini
Outcome
The engineer used the findings tracker to refine the AI engine's RAG pipeline before customer release, separate from functional bug reports and feature input

01Context

Parallaxlogic Infotech builds an OSINT (open-source intelligence) threat-intelligence platform that pulls from public channels, groups, and stories. It includes a new in-house AI module that generates 0-100 threat scores, red flags, and narrative summaries.

My employer allowed me to describe how I audited those outputs, without showing the platform or any real records.

02My role

I audited the AI module's outputs and logged failures so the engineer could refine the RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) pipeline. Separately, I tested the platform for functional software bugs, reported them to engineering, and suggested product improvements. I built the AI-inconsistency findings tracker myself using Gemini. I did not design, train, or build the model.

03Approach

Step 1

Auditing scores, flags and quote context

I checked each 0-100 threat score, red flag, and summary against the raw public posts it cited, looking for hallucinations, broken logic, and deterministic extraction or NLP errors. A big part of this was reading cited quotes in context to see whether they expressed real personal intent or were reposted news, sarcasm, or gaming talk, and checking if the source evidence contradicted flags the system marked as positive.

Decision

Reading the full thread around a quote caught false positives that looked alarming in isolation.

Step 2

Checking demographic sources and cross-case links

For demographic fields (language, timezone, and location), I checked whether the attribution came from the posts themselves or from gazetteer heuristics. On cross-case links, I checked whether two profiles had interacted directly or only appeared together through a shared public channel, a moderation bot, or a self-match.

Decision

I checked every location against the posts themselves, and every link between profiles against actual interaction between them. This stopped two false results from being reported as fact: a location guessed from a place-name lookup, and two unrelated profiles linked only because both used the same channel bot.

Step 3

Tracking AI inconsistencies separately from functional bugs

Using Gemini, I put together a Google Sheets tracker for AI inconsistencies so the engineer could review individual failures and refine the RAG pipeline before customer release. Each row logged the finding title, assessment, verdict, profile reference, and evidence. Functional software bugs went into a separate engineering report, along with my suggestions for improving the platform.

Trade-off

Keeping two logs meant classifying every issue up front, so RAG and training-data feedback did not get mixed up with routine software bugs.

04Data governance and constraints

Because the platform handles threat-intelligence assessments, I left several details out of this write-up.

Confidentiality and scope boundaries

Governance rules
Employer permission
Parallaxlogic Infotech allowed me to describe my evaluation method and tracker structure, without showing the platform.
Platform and source anonymity
Neither the OSINT platform nor the specific social or messaging platforms it collects from are named.
No live records or identifiers
No internal URLs, IP addresses, hostnames, real names, real profiles, channel names, or dossier contents appear anywhere on this site.
Fictional tracker entries
The findings tracker table is shown only as an illustrative template with fictional entries.

05Outcome

The engineer used the findings tracker to review failure cases and tighten the RAG pipeline before customer release.

In parallel, engineering received a separate bug log for functional defects alongside my suggestions for product improvements.

06What I learned

I can spot where the model gets an output wrong, even if I cannot yet explain the statistical reason it produced that error. Closing that gap is one reason I want formal graduate training.

07Tools and method

Google Sheets and Gemini.

I built the findings tracker template with Gemini. All audit reviews, failure classifications, and evidence checks were my own.
